Toilet Repair & Installation in Windsor, CO

Running tanks, weak flushes, leaks at the base and toilets that clog again. The fault is found first, and the toilet is replaced only when repair no longer makes sense.

  • Flapper and fill valve fixes
  • Wax ring and flange repair
  • Toilet replacement
  • Clog diagnosis
Symptoms

A toilet that runs, rocks, leaks or clogs

A tank that fills and refills on its own, a weak flush, water around the base, a toilet that rocks, and a bowl that clogs often are the common faults. A running toilet wastes water quietly, which is how it shows up on the water bill.

Toilet repair swaps worn parts: the flapper, fill valve, wax ring and bolts. Toilet installation sets a new toilet on a clean flange with a fresh seal.

Replacement

What a toilet replacement involves

  1. 1

    Choose the toilet

    Rough-in distance, usually 12 inches, and height are checked.

  2. 2

    Remove the old toilet

    The water is shut off, the tank drained, and the toilet lifted off the flange.

  3. 3

    Check the flange

    A cracked or low flange is repaired so the new seal can hold.

  4. 4

    Set the new toilet

    A new wax ring or seal and bolts are used, and the toilet is leveled.

  5. 5

    Test

    The supply is connected, the tank filled and the toilet flushed to check for leaks.

Windsor

Water use and toilet choices in Windsor

Federal rules and the International Plumbing Code (IPC) cap new toilets at 1.6 gallons per flush, and WaterSense models use 1.28 or less. The Town bills water on a tiered rate that rose on February 1, 2026, so a toilet that runs wastes more than it did a year ago. A dye test in the tank is the quickest check for a leaking flapper.

Toilets in basement bathrooms may be fed by an ejector pump instead of gravity. Those are a separate setup, and basement bathrooms are covered under basement plumbing. Like-for-like replacement is routine work, and the Town's permit technician at 970-674-2436 can say whether a permit applies to a given job.

Overflow

What to do if a toilet is overflowing

  • ✓Lift the tank lid and push the flapper down to stop the fill.
  • ✓Close the shutoff valve behind or beside the toilet.
  • ✓Do not flush again, and keep children and pets away.
  • ✓Soak up water with towels, and wear gloves if it is sewage.
  • ✓If other fixtures are backing up too, stop using water and call.
