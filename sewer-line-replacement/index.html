When

When a sewer line needs replacing instead of repairing

A new sewer line is the answer once the old one has stopped being worth repairing. That is the case when damage spreads along much of the run, when a section has collapsed, or when the material itself is failing. Repeated spot repairs in one line, a long sag that holds water, and Orangeburg pipe (bituminized fiber, found in many homes built from the 1940s to the early 1970s) are the usual triggers.

Sewer line replacement means installing a new line from the house connection to the main, either by pulling new pipe through the old path or by digging a trench. Spot fixes and linings are covered under sewer line repair. New plastic sewer pipe is commonly PVC made to ASTM D3034, the specification for PVC sewer pipe and fittings.

Process

What a replacement job involves

  1. 1

    Inspect and scope

    Camera footage and a locator establish the length, depth and route.

  2. 2

    Locates and permits

    811 marks public utilities, a private locate finds the sewer line, and the Town's permit rules are checked.

  3. 3

    Prepare the work area

    Pits or the trench are laid out, with sprinklers, trees and the driveway noted.

  4. 4

    Install the new line

    New pipe goes in at a continuous slope and is connected at the house and at the main.

  5. 5

    Test and restore

    The line is camera-checked or tested, the pit or trench is backfilled, and the surface is restored.

Ask what the Town requires for the connection at the main and who inspects it. Get the answer in writing before the work is scheduled.

Windsor

Windsor rules that shape a sewer line replacement

The Town requires a building permit to install or move plumbing, even when the owner does the work, and it requires service providers to notify it of work in the sanitary sewer system. Plumbing work also follows the Colorado Plumbing Code, which builds on the International Plumbing Code (IPC). Call Town Hall at 970-674-2400 before the project is scheduled to confirm what applies to your address.

Colorado requires a call to 811 at least two business days before digging. 811 marks public utilities and not your private sewer line, so a private locate is usually part of the plan.

On the Larimer County side of Windsor the Town provides storm sewer only, so the sewer arrangement can differ from the Town's. Confirm that before you compare bids.

Frost depth is the other local number. The Town's code sets the frost line at 30 inches, and buried lines normally sit below that depth.

Decision

Repair or replace: a decision test

Repair or line when

  • Damage is limited to one or two sections
  • The pipe is otherwise sound on camera
  • There are no long sags or collapses
  • The line is a modern material

Replace when

  • Damage runs along much of the line
  • The pipe has collapsed or is Orangeburg
  • A long sag holds water
  • Repairs have repeated in the same line
