Emergencies

What counts as a plumbing emergency in Windsor

Some plumbing problems can wait for a morning appointment and some cannot. If water is spreading, sewage is coming up, or a leak will not shut off, treat it as an emergency and call an emergency plumber right away.

Services

Plumbing work we handle around Windsor

Water heaters

A tank that leaks, rumbles, or runs out of hot water usually needs water heater repair or a swap. Households that want continuous hot water can look at tankless water heater service.

Drains and sewer lines

One clogged fixture is a drain cleaning job. Backups that keep returning point to the main line, where a camera shows the cause and hydro jetting clears roots and grease.

Hidden leaks

A meter that keeps turning, a warm patch on the floor, or a stain that keeps growing all mean water is escaping somewhere. Leak detection locates it before walls or floors are opened.

Winter freeze damage

Cold snaps split pipes in garages, crawl spaces and exterior walls. Frozen pipe repair thaws and fixes the damaged run. When a pipe has already burst, emergency pipe repair stops the water and replaces the section.

After the water stops

A burst pipe or sewer backup leaves wet materials behind. Water damage restoration covers extraction, drying and moisture readings, so repairs are not sealing in damp framing.

Water and gas lines

A failing service line between the meter and the house needs water line repair or replacement. New appliance hookups and repairs to existing gas piping fall under gas line installation and repair.

Local

What is different about plumbing in Windsor

Windsor sits in Weld and Larimer counties, buys treated water from three providers, and grew fast after a 2008 tornado. Each of those facts changes how a plumbing call plays out.

Three water providers

The Town of Windsor serves Weld County addresses, Fort Collins-Loveland Water District serves Larimer County addresses, and North Weld County Water District serves Harmony Ridge and Alexander Estates. The Town buys its water, according to its own water quality report: about 30 percent from the City of Greeley and about 70 percent from the two districts.

This matters in a leak. A meter that keeps turning with every fixture closed usually means water is escaping on the house side of the meter, and your bill tells you which provider to call about a break in the street. For a main break at a Town of Windsor address, the after-hours line is 970-674-5415.

A town that grew after 2008

An EF3 tornado crossed Windsor on May 22, 2008, damaging roughly 850 homes, nearly 300 of them heavily damaged or destroyed. The town has about doubled in population since, so a large share of homes are newer than the storm.

Newer homes tend to bring water heater, fixture and sewer-line problems. Blocks near Main Street, platted in 1882, are where original supply lines and old sewer laterals show their age, which is when repiping or sewer line repair enters the conversation.

A 30-inch frost line

Windsor amends the International Building Code and International Residential Code locally, and one of its amendments sets the frost line at 30 inches. Buried mains normally sit below that depth.

The pipes that split in a cold snap are the ones above that line: hose bibs, runs in garages and exterior walls, and crawl space lines. Disconnecting hoses before the first hard freeze and knowing where your main shutoff is covers most of the risk.

Permits and HOA irrigation

The Town requires a building permit to install or move plumbing, even when the owner does the work. Installs also follow the Colorado Plumbing Code, which builds on the International Plumbing Code (IPC). Town inspections are handled by its contractor, SAFEbuilt.

Many newer subdivisions also have a separate non-potable irrigation system run by an HOA or metro district. The Town does not switch those on or off, so a leaking sprinkler line starts with a call to the HOA.
