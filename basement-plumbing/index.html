Floor drains, sewage ejector pumps, basement bathrooms and the pipes that run through a basement ceiling. Basements are common in Windsor, and so are their plumbing problems.

Why it matters

Basements are where Windsor's plumbing problems show up first

Basements are common across Windsor and Northern Colorado, and they hold the lowest fixtures in the house. A blocked main shows up first at a basement floor drain, groundwater shows up at the sump, and a leak above shows up on the ceiling. That makes the basement the place to look first.

Basement plumbing covers the pipes, drains and pumps below grade: floor drains and their traps, sewage ejector pumps for basement bathrooms, the water heater and supply lines in the utility room, and the connection to the sewer line. Each of these parts fails in its own way, so a basement call starts with a look at all four.

The International Plumbing Code (IPC) requires every trap to keep a water seal, which is why a floor drain that rarely sees water needs a trap primer. A basement floor drain that backs up is the most urgent sign. It often means a blocked main, and drain cleaning is the first step.

Emergency

Emergency response for basement flooding and sewage backups

A basement floods quickly because every drain in the house sits above it. Call the 24/7 line if a floor drain is overflowing, an ejector pit is full or water is coming up around the toilet. Stay out of standing water if outlets or the panel are nearby.

The emergency visit stops the source first, which may mean clearing the line, restoring the pump or shutting off a failed fixture. Pumping and drying are separate jobs, and the crew will tell you which ones you need.

Windsor

Rules and soil that shape Windsor basements

The Town's sewer rules state that the building sewer should be brought to the building below the basement floor where possible, and that where a building drain is too low for gravity flow, sewage must be lifted by an approved means. That is a sewage ejector pump, and it is a different machine from a sump pump. Both belong to basement plumbing, and they must never share a discharge.

The Colorado Geological Survey identifies expansive soil as a major geologic hazard in the state, and clay moving around a foundation can pull on pipes that pass through the basement wall. Water in the pit is a groundwater question for sump pump installation and repair. Windsor Municipal Code also bars sump discharge across sidewalks and streets, and groundwater from any sanitary sewer.

Basement calls come from homes near Windsor Lake and Boardwalk Park, along the river corridor at Eastman Park, and from newer neighborhoods such as Water Valley and Highland Meadows. A look at the sump, the floor drain and the water heater covers the most common causes in any of them.

Bathrooms

What adding a basement bathroom involves

  1. 1

    Check the sewer elevation

    The basement drain height versus the sewer line decides gravity flow or an ejector.

  2. 2

    Permit and plan

    Adding a fixture requires a Town permit, and plans show drains, vents and supply.

  3. 3

    Rough-in

    Drain, vent and supply lines are run, with the slab opened where needed.

  4. 4

    Install the ejector

    If needed, a sealed ejector basin and pump are set, vented and discharged to the sewer line.

  5. 5

    Inspect and finish

    Rough and final inspections are passed before the walls and floor are closed.

Flooding

Emergency steps when the basement floor drain backs up

  • ✓Stop using water everywhere in the house.
  • ✓Keep out of the basement if water is near outlets, the panel or appliances.
  • ✓Avoid contact with sewage, and keep children and pets away.
  • ✓Move belongings up off the floor, and photograph the water line.
  • ✓Skip chemical cleaners, and call for the main line to be cleared.
