What it is

What water mitigation means

Water mitigation is the emergency stage of water damage work. The goal is to stop the damage from growing: shut off or contain the source, pull out standing water, move or protect contents, and start drying before materials swell, warp or grow mold.

Repair of the source follows the International Plumbing Code (IPC), and restoration comes after. It is the repair and rebuild of whatever mitigation could not save, and it is covered on the page for water damage restoration. Mitigation is the part that cannot wait for an appointment.

The EPA advises drying wet materials within 24 to 48 hours to keep mold from starting, which is why mitigation is measured in hours and not days. Wet drywall, carpet pad and insulation are the materials that need attention first.

First hour

What to do before help arrives

  • ✓Stop the source if you can: close the main shutoff for a plumbing leak.
  • ✓Keep out of any room where water has reached outlets, cords or the panel.
  • ✓Lift furniture, rugs and electronics clear of the wet floor.
  • ✓Take photos and video of the damage before anything is moved.
  • ✓Leave cabinet and closet doors open so air reaches wet walls.
  • ✓Do not run household vacuums or ceiling fans over wet surfaces.
Windsor

Where mitigation calls come from in Windsor

Burst pipes in winter, water heater failures in basements, sewer backups, and failed sump pumps during spring runoff are the common sources. Windsor's flood insurance study ties major flooding in the Cache la Poudre valley to heavy local rain combined with snowmelt, which is also when basements take on groundwater.

Basements make mitigation more urgent. Water that reaches a finished basement wicks into drywall and carpet within hours, and it collects at the lowest point. A failed sump pump is the usual culprit when groundwater is the source.

What affects the cost of mitigation

Mitigation is priced by the size of the affected area, the water category and the equipment days. Clean water from a supply line costs less to handle than sewage.
