Honest start

Check your hardness before you buy a softener

Hardness is dissolved calcium and magnesium. It leaves scale in water heaters and on fixtures, and it reduces how well soap works. The Water Quality Association classes water as soft up to about 3.5 grains per gallon, moderate to about 7, and harder beyond that.

The numbers floating around online for Windsor-area towns are not reliable. The Town of Windsor's own water quality report does not list hardness, and vendor pages quote figures for the same towns that differ several-fold. A test of your own tap water, or your provider's current figure, settles the question before any equipment is chosen.

Scale

What hardness means for a home

Water hardness ranges, their classification and the usual response
Range (grains per gallon)ClassificationUsual response
0 to 3.5SoftNo softener needed. Flush the water heater on schedule.
3.5 to 7ModerateA softener is optional. Scale builds slowly, and a flush schedule helps.
7 to 10.5HardA softener is commonly worthwhile, particularly with a tankless heater.
Over 10.5Very hardA softener is usually recommended.

The ranges follow the Water Quality Association scale. Moderate water often does not justify a softener, but it does justify a flush schedule for water heater repair prevention.

Installation

What a softener installation involves

  1. 1

    Test the water

    Hardness, and iron if present, are measured so the unit is sized correctly.

  2. 2

    Choose capacity

    Grain capacity is sized to the household's daily use and the measured hardness.

  3. 3

    Plumb the unit

    Inlet, outlet, a bypass valve and a drain line are installed. A power outlet and a floor drain or standpipe are needed, and the International Plumbing Code (IPC) calls for the drain to discharge through an air gap.

  4. 4

    Set regeneration

    The control valve is programmed for hardness, salt dose and schedule.

  5. 5

    Load salt and verify

    Salt is added, a regeneration is run, and treated water is tested at a faucet.

A new softener is plumbing work, so the Town's rule that plumbing installs need a building permit is worth asking about. The permit desk can say what the install needs before any work begins.

Windsor

What is different about softening in Windsor

Windsor's drinking water comes through a mix of suppliers. The Town buys about 30 percent from the City of Greeley and about 70 percent from Fort Collins-Loveland Water District and North Weld County Water District, so the water at a tap can reflect that blend. Larimer County addresses are served directly by the district, and a few subdivisions by North Weld.

Softeners also discharge brine to the drain. Check HOA and municipal rules on brine, and be aware that softening adds sodium to the water. Households on sodium-restricted diets should ask a doctor, and a doctor can say whether it matters for you. If the issue is taste, chlorine or lead and not hardness, water filtration installation is the better answer.
