Replace or repair

When a water heater should be replaced instead of repaired

A tank that leaks through its body cannot be patched, because the steel liner has failed. Rust-colored hot water, a growing puddle at the base, and a unit well past a decade old all point toward replacement. The Department of Energy puts the typical life of a storage tank at 10 to 15 years.

A younger unit with a failed element, thermostat or gas valve is usually worth fixing through water heater repair. A major repair on a unit in its second decade rarely pays back, so weigh the repair bill against the unit's age.

Water heater types and where each fits in a Windsor home

Most Windsor homes have a gas or electric tank in a basement or utility room. Federal efficiency rules in place since 2015 mean most electric tanks above 55 gallons are now heat pump models.

Installation

What a proper replacement includes

The tank is only part of the work. Code details are where a cheap install and a good one differ.

  1. 1

    Permit and plan

    The Town of Windsor requires a building permit to install or move plumbing, so the job is planned around it.

  2. 2

    Shut down and drain

    Water, power or gas is isolated, and the old tank is drained and disconnected.

  3. 3

    Set the new unit

    The heater sits level, in a drain pan where a leak would damage finished space, with new water connections.

  4. 4

    Safety fittings

    The International Plumbing Code (IPC) calls for a temperature and pressure relief valve with a discharge pipe, and a thermal expansion tank is added where the home has a closed water system.

  5. 5

    Gas, venting and test

    Gas connections get a leak check, venting is verified, and the unit is fired and tested.

Garages add one more rule. Fuel-burning water heaters there generally need their ignition source raised about 18 inches off the floor and protected from vehicle impact.

Before the visit

What to do before a water heater replacement

  • ✓Photograph the label on the old heater so the fuel type, size and model are on record.
  • ✓Find the cold water shutoff on the heater and the main shutoff for the house, and confirm both turn.
  • ✓Clear a path and about three feet of working space around the unit.
  • ✓Note where the gas shutoff or the electrical breaker for the heater is.
  • ✓If the heater is leaking, close the cold inlet and cut power or gas now, before anyone arrives.
  • ✓Smell of gas at any point means leave the house and call 911 first.
Local

Sizing and placement for Windsor households

A household of three or four people is typically served by a 40 to 50 gallon tank. Larger families, soaking tubs and back-to-back showers call for 50 to 75 gallons or a tankless unit. The first-hour rating on the label matters as much as the tank size.

Most Windsor basements and utility rooms already have a floor drain, which makes relief valve and pan drains simple to run. Closet installs have limited clearance, so measure the space before choosing a taller power-vent or heat pump model.

The thermostat is the last setting to get right. Setting it at 120 degrees Fahrenheit, as the Department of Energy advises for most households, keeps scald risk and standby heat loss down.

The Town of Windsor requires a building permit to install or move plumbing, and a new water heater counts. The permit technician can be reached at 970-674-2436, and inspections are handled by SAFEbuilt. Pulling the permit is part of the job and not an extra step for you to chase.

A new tank installation includes a temperature and pressure relief valve with a discharge pipe run to a safe point, a shutoff on the cold line and a drain pan where a leak would damage finished space. Where the house has a closed water system, an expansion tank absorbs the pressure that heating water creates. These parts are in the code because a failed tank without them becomes a flood.

Gas heaters need combustion air and a vent sized to the appliance, and at 4,800 feet the burner is set to altitude specifications. Electric heaters need the right breaker and wire gauge for their elements. Changing fuel type or moving the heater changes the scope of the job, and the quote should say so before work starts.

Ask what happens to the old tank before the visit. Disposal is part of the quote for some jobs and an extra for others, and a corroded shutoff valve is far easier to replace while the old heater is already out.

Think about how the water is actually used. A household that runs a dishwasher and a shower at once needs recovery speed and not only tank size, and the first-hour rating on the yellow EnergyGuide label shows what the heater can deliver. Ask for the manufacture date on the new tank as well, because the serial number encodes it and checking takes a minute at delivery.

Replacements are scheduled across town, from older homes near Main Street to RainDance, Water Valley and Highland Meadows. The right tank or tankless unit depends on the household's hot water use and the space available, not on which neighborhood the house is in.

Pricing

What changes the price of a water heater replacement

A like-for-like swap is the smallest job. Changing fuel type, moving the unit, or upgrading to heat pump or tankless adds gas, electrical, venting or drain work.

Unit typeTank, power-vent, heat pump and tankless prices differ widely.
CapacityGallon size and first-hour rating set the model.
VentingRe-using a good flue costs less than new venting.
PermitPermit fees and inspection steps belong in the quote.
RemovalAsk whether disposal of the old tank is in the quote.

A failing tank can flood a basement. Homeowner policies commonly cover water damage from a sudden leak but not the water heater itself, so replacing an aging unit before it fails is the cheaper path.
