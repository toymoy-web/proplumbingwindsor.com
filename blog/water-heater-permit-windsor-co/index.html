Water Heater Permit in Windsor, CO: Check Before You Swap

A water heater swap feels routine, but permits, inspections and who does the work are rules, not suggestions. Here is what to ask the Town before the old tank comes out.

By the Windsor Pro Plumbing team. Published October 2, 2026.

The rule

What the Town of Windsor says about plumbing permits

The Town of Windsor's published guidance is that a building permit is required to install or move plumbing, even when the owner does the work. For permit questions, the Town directs people to its permit technician at 970-674-2436. A water heater replacement is an installation, so the safe assumption is that it needs a permit unless the Town tells you otherwise.

Permit summaries from third-party sites list water heater replacement as permit-required for Windsor, and many Colorado jurisdictions treat it the same way. None of that replaces a call to the Town, which can confirm in one conversation.

Who

Who is allowed to do the work in Colorado

Colorado law requires plumbing work to be done by a licensed plumber, with an exception for a homeowner working on a home they occupy. A homeowner doing their own water heater is legal where the exception applies, but the permit and inspection rules still apply.

A contractor should pull the permit and appear on it as the plumber of record, taking responsibility for work that meets the code the Town has adopted, built on the International Plumbing Code (IPC). Be cautious of anyone who asks you to pull the permit yourself on their behalf, since that can be a sign the work is not licensed. The next post covers how to check a plumber's license.

Safety

Details an inspector will look at

  • ✓A temperature and pressure relief valve with a discharge pipe that ends in a safe place.
  • ✓A thermal expansion tank where the home has a closed water system.
  • ✓Proper venting and combustion air for gas units, and the right clearances.
  • ✓Correct gas piping, a shutoff valve and a sediment trap on gas heaters.
  • ✓In a garage, the burner or ignition source raised about 18 inches and protected from vehicles.
The process

What a permitted water heater swap looks like

  1. 1

    Confirm the rule

    Call the Town's permit technician at 970-674-2436 and describe the job.

  2. 2

    Pull the permit

    The plumber of record applies, and the permit fee is part of the job cost.

  3. 3

    Do the work

    Old heater out, new one set with relief valve, expansion tank if needed, and venting.

  4. 4

    Schedule the inspection

    The Town's inspection contractor checks the installation.

  5. 5

    Keep the paperwork

    File the permit and inspection sign-off with your home records.

Records

Documents worth keeping

Those four items answer almost every future question, from a warranty claim to a buyer's inspector asking when the heater was replaced.

  • ✓The permit number and the final inspection sign-off.
  • ✓The new heater's model and serial number and the warranty card.
  • ✓The invoice, with the date and the plumber's license number.
  • ✓A photo of the installed heater with its relief valve and discharge pipe.
Tank to tankless

Extra steps when converting from a tank to tankless

Swapping a tank for a tankless unit is more than a heater change. Tankless units draw far more gas than a tank, so the gas line size has to be checked and sometimes upsized. Venting is different, often plastic pipe through a wall in place of a metal flue, and condensing models need a drain for acidic condensate.

A power supply is needed for the control board and fan, and the water lines usually get service valves for flushing. All of that is inspected, which is why a conversion is a bigger permit conversation than a like-for-like tank swap.

Safety devices

Alarms and the relief valve

Colorado requires carbon monoxide alarms in most homes with fuel-burning appliances, and the water heater is one of them. Check that the alarms near sleeping areas work after any gas appliance work. The relief valve discharge pipe must run to a safe location, not be capped or reduced, since it is the last safeguard against over-pressure.

Ask the installer to show you the relief valve, the shutoff and the expansion tank when the job is done. Knowing where they are is half of knowing what to do in an emergency, and it takes two minutes.

Selling

Why an unpermitted swap can cost you later

Work done without a permit can surface during a home sale, a refinance or an insurance claim. A buyer's inspector may ask for the permit, and an unpermitted installation may need to be opened for inspection later. A permit is cheap insurance against that.

If your heater is failing, the planning for a replacement lives on the page for water heater installation and replacement, and a tank that is only misbehaving may be a water heater repair job. Homeowners in Milliken should call the Town of Milliken, since each town sets its own permit rules.
