Burst Pipe Insurance Claim in Colorado: What to Document

A burst pipe is usually a covered event, and a claim is only as good as its paperwork. Here is what to do and keep from the first hour.

By the Windsor Pro Plumbing team. Published October 2, 2026.

The rule of thumb

Sudden and accidental is the phrase that matters

Homeowner policies commonly distinguish sudden and accidental water damage, such as a burst pipe or a failed water heater, from gradual leaks and wear. The first is usually covered, and the second often is not. Policies differ, so the policy language and your agent decide any specific claim.

Insurers also expect reasonable steps to limit further damage. That means stopping the water, starting drying, and keeping receipts.

First hour

What to document

  • ✓Photograph and video the source, the water and every affected room before moving things.
  • ✓Keep the failed pipe section, labeled, if it is easy to do so.
  • ✓Save the plumber's invoice and ask that it state the cause and the failed part.
  • ✓Keep receipts for emergency extraction, drying and repairs.
  • ✓Keep damaged items until the adjuster has seen them, and list them with ages and values.
  • ✓Write down dates, times and who you spoke to at the insurer.
Drying

Why drying paperwork matters

Moisture readings and drying logs show that water was dealt with properly. They also help show that the damage did not grow after the event. If the drying is done by a restoration company, ask for the readings in writing. The process is described on the page for water damage restoration.

Mold is the other reason to move fast. The EPA advises drying wet materials within 24 to 48 hours, and a claim that waits a week can be disputed over damage that grew.

Adjuster

Working with the adjuster

The adjuster inspects the damage and writes an estimate, and the estimate may differ from your contractor's. Show the adjuster everything, including the documents above and the plumber's note on cause. If the numbers differ, ask for the adjuster's line items so you can compare them to your contractor's quote.

Do not sign a release or accept a final payment until you understand the scope, and ask what covers hidden damage found during repairs. Supplements to a claim are common when drywall comes off and more is found.

Mitigation

Mitigation versus repair costs

Emergency steps such as extraction, drying and board-up are often reimbursed as part of the claim. Repairs and rebuilding follow once the adjuster approves a scope. Keeping the two sets of receipts apart helps both you and the insurer.

If you disagree with a decision, the Colorado Division of Insurance, which sits inside the Department of Regulatory Agencies, accepts consumer questions and complaints. Keep your records in order, and keep a log of every call.

Denial

If a claim is denied or underpaid

Ask for the denial in writing with the policy language it relies on. Compare it to your policy and your documents. The Colorado Division of Insurance takes consumer complaints, and a public adjuster or an attorney can review a disputed claim. Appraisal clauses in some policies provide a process for disagreements about amount.

A denial for a gradual leak is common when records suggest the damage was long-running. A plumber's note that the failure was sudden, with photos of the failed section, is the best counter.

Plumbing

The repair side of the claim

Stop the leak first through burst pipe repair, then handle the claim. A plumber's note describing the cause, the pipe type and the age of the failure gives the adjuster a clean picture of a sudden event and not a gradual leak.

Colorado homeowners with a dispute can contact the Colorado Division of Insurance, part of the Department of Regulatory Agencies. In fast-growing places like Timnath, many policies are new, so read the exclusions before you need them.
