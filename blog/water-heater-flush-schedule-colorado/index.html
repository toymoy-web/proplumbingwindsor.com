Water Heater Flush Schedule for Colorado Homes, Step by Step

A yearly flush slows sediment and gives you an early look at the tank's condition. It is simple, but it needs a few safety steps in the right order.

By the Windsor Pro Plumbing team. Published October 2, 2026.

Why

What flushing does

Sediment settles on the floor of a tank heater over time. It insulates the water from the burner or element, makes popping and rumbling noises, and speeds wear. Flushing carries some of that sediment out through the drain valve.

How fast sediment builds depends on the water. The Town of Windsor's report does not publish hardness, and the Town buys water from three suppliers, so the safest schedule is a yearly flush and an adjustment based on what comes out.

Schedule

A flush and maintenance schedule

Water heater maintenance tasks, how often to do them and why
TaskHow oftenWhy
Quick drain of a few gallonsEvery 3 to 6 months if you likeChecks for sediment and keeps the valve working
Full tank flushAbout once a yearRemoves settled sediment
Test the relief valveOnce a year, carefullyConfirms the safety valve moves
Inspect the anode rodEvery few yearsA spent rod lets the tank corrode
Tankless descaleAbout once a year, or per the manualClears scale from the heat exchanger
How to

How to flush a tank water heater

Fill the tank completely before restoring power. An electric element turned on dry can burn out in seconds.

  1. 1

    Turn off the power or gas

    For electric units, switch off the breaker. For gas units, turn the control to pilot or off.

  2. 2

    Let the water cool

    Hot water scalds. Wait an hour or two, or run hot water elsewhere until it cools.

  3. 3

    Connect a hose

    Attach a garden hose to the drain valve and run it to a floor drain or outdoors.

  4. 4

    Open the valve and a hot faucet

    Opening a hot faucet upstairs lets air in so the tank drains. Watch the water for grit.

  5. 5

    Flush and close

    Briefly open the cold inlet to stir sediment, drain until the water runs clear, close the valve, refill the tank fully, and then restore power or gas.

Stop signs

When to stop and call a plumber

  • ✓The drain valve will not open, or it leaks after closing.
  • ✓The water runs rust-colored or full of chunks.
  • ✓The relief valve discharge drips or the tank body is wet.
  • ✓There is a gas smell. Leave the house and call 911 first.
  • ✓The tank is more than 10 to 15 years old and already noisy.
Settings

The temperature setting is part of maintenance

The Department of Energy suggests setting the thermostat at 120 degrees Fahrenheit for most households. That limits scald risk and standby heat loss. If the dial is higher, lower it after a flush when the tank is full.

A heater that fails a flush may be at the end of its life. Problems found during a flush are covered by water heater repair, and a tank past its age is a replacement question for water heater installation and replacement.

Anode

The anode rod, the quiet protector

An anode rod hangs inside the tank and corrodes in place of the steel. It is made of magnesium or aluminum, and it wears down over years. A rod that is nearly gone lets the tank begin to rust, which is a leading reason tanks fail early.

Many plumbers check the rod every few years. Replacing a spent rod can extend a tank's life, though a tank already rusting inside is past help. Softened water can use up a rod faster, so ask about it if you add a softener.

Fuel type

What differs for gas and electric heaters

Gas heaters

  • Shut the gas control to off before draining
  • Check the pilot and the flue draft after refill
  • Sediment sits under the burner and causes popping
  • Watch for any gas smell

Electric heaters

  • Switch off the breaker before draining
  • Refill the tank completely before power returns
  • Sediment covers the lower element
  • A burnt element is a common failure after neglect
Sounds

Reading the sounds a tank makes

Popping or rumbling means water trapped under sediment is boiling and bubbling through it. A high-pitched whine can mean a restriction, and a sizzle may be water dripping on a hot element or burner. A ticking noise is usually pipes expanding as water heats.

A new noise is a signal worth acting on. If a flush quiets the tank, the sediment was the cause. If it does not, the tank may be near the end of its life.

Log

A simple maintenance log

Write the install date and model on the tank with a marker, and keep a note of each flush, relief valve test and repair. The log tells you how old the tank is, and the age is the most useful number when you decide between repair and replacement.

Most storage tanks last 10 to 15 years, according to the Department of Energy. Starting at year ten, plan the replacement so a failure is a scheduled swap and not a flood.

Local

Flush timing in a cold climate

Fall is a good time. It is far easier to find a failing valve in October than on a January morning. In Milliken and other towns with many older homes, an annual flush also gives an early read on whether an aging tank is worth keeping.
