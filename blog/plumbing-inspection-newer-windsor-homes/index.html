Plumbing Checklist for Newer Windsor, CO Homes (Built 2008+)

Newer homes do not have old pipes, but they do have setup details that decide how they age. Here are the ones worth checking.

By the Windsor Pro Plumbing team. Published October 2, 2026.

Context

Why Windsor has so many newer homes

An EF3 tornado crossed Windsor on May 22, 2008, damaging about 850 homes, and the town has roughly doubled in population since. A large share of the housing stock is newer than the storm, and much of it was built in a short window of fast growth.

New homes bring their own checklist. The pipes are often modern plastic, and the usual problems are less about wear and more about how things were set up at the start.

Windsor

Details specific to this area

Windsor's code bars sump pump discharge across driveways, sidewalks, curbs and streets, so check that the discharge line goes to a lawful outlet. Many newer subdivisions also have a non-potable irrigation system run by an HOA, and the Town does not switch those on or off. Know which you have.

The Town buys its water from three suppliers, and the blend varies, so hardness and taste can differ from a neighboring subdivision. A water test before buying a softener is cheaper than assuming. Irrigation backflow devices in newer subdivisions are worth testing on a schedule, which is covered under backflow testing and prevention.

Closing

If you are buying

  • ✓Ask for the permit history on the water heater and any basement finishing.
  • ✓Order a sewer camera inspection, since it takes minutes and reveals sags and cracks.
  • ✓Test the main shutoff and every fixture stop at least once.
  • ✓Check the water heater age on its label, and plan replacement if it is near ten years.
  • ✓Look for stains on basement ceilings and under sinks.
Parts

Builder-grade parts that tend to fail first

Production builders choose parts for price and speed. Supply stops under sinks, toilet fill valves, hose bibs and kitchen faucets are the usual early failures. None is expensive, and none is a sign of a bad house. They are consumables that wear out sooner than the pipes behind the walls.

A walk-through with a flashlight and a dry paper towel in the first year, then every few years, finds weeping valves and slow drips before they stain a cabinet. Replace braided supply lines about every 5 to 10 years.

Pressure

Why water pressure deserves a gauge

Pressure reducing valves cost little and fail quietly. A gauge on a hose bib reads static pressure in seconds. The International Plumbing Code limits it to 80 psi in a building, and a house sitting at 100 or more is stressing every fixture, hose and heater. A new valve is a small repair that can prevent a large one.

Pressure also explains hammering pipes and weeping fixtures in newer subdivisions on the district side of town. A gauge settles the question, and it takes less time than the debate.

Annual

A yearly plumbing check for a newer home

  • ✓Test the main shutoff and the sump pump.
  • ✓Flush a few gallons from the water heater drain.
  • ✓Check under every sink for dampness.
  • ✓Inspect hose bibs and disconnect hoses before winter.
  • ✓Read the pressure gauge and look at the pressure reducing valve.
  • ✓Confirm the irrigation backflow device was tested.
Inspector gaps

What a general home inspection may not cover

A standard inspection is visual and brief. It may not include a sewer camera, a pressure reading, a sump pump load test or the irrigation backflow device. If the report says plumbing is functional, ask which of those were checked.

Add the missing checks yourself or through a plumber. They take little time and sit in the gap between what an inspector is paid to look at and what costs money later.

Spring

Spring checks for hose bibs and irrigation

After winter, open each outdoor faucet slowly and check for a drip at the handle or inside the wall. Turn the irrigation on zone by zone and look at each head. In a subdivision with a shared non-potable system, the HOA starts the main, and you check your own zones.

Local note

What newer Windsor builds have in common

Windsor has grown quickly, with many homes built from the 2000s onward. Builder-grade fixtures, PEX supply lines and shared subdivision utilities are common.

Newer does not mean maintenance free. Check shutoffs, hose bibs and the water heater before a warranty runs out. Nearby pages: Severance and Timnath. Related service: repiping.

Next

When a checklist item needs a plumber

A failed relief valve or leaking heater is a job for water heater repair. A missing backflow tag is a job for a certified tester. Homes in fast-growing towns like Timnath share the same checklist. Treat it as routine maintenance for the first decade, and it will not turn into a repair list.
