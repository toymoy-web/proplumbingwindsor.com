Non-Potable Irrigation Water in Windsor, CO: HOA Rules

Many newer Windsor subdivisions have a second water system that is not for drinking. Knowing who runs it saves a lot of confusion when a sprinkler line leaks.

By the Windsor Pro Plumbing team. Published October 2, 2026.

The basics

What non-potable irrigation water is

Non-potable water is untreated or partly treated water used for irrigation, not for drinking, bathing or cooking. In many newer developments, the Town of Windsor requires a secondary non-potable system so lawns are watered from a separate source and not from treated drinking water. A leak in a purple irrigation line is found like any other, through leak detection, and the repair stays on the irrigation side.

Those systems have their own pipes, valves and meter, and they are usually managed by an HOA or metro district. The Town's water and sewer maintenance staff are not responsible for turning HOA non-potable systems on or off, according to the Town's own page. For that, the Town tells residents to consult their HOA.

Safety

What to do and what not to do around non-potable water

  • ✓Never connect non-potable lines to indoor plumbing or a drinking water tap.
  • ✓Do not drink from outdoor faucets or hose bibs connected to a non-potable system.
  • ✓Look for labels, and note that non-potable lines are often purple or marked as reclaimed or non-potable.
  • ✓Keep backflow devices on irrigation current, and test them on schedule.
  • ✓Tell guests and children which spigots are not for drinking.
Backflow

Why backflow devices matter on irrigation

Backflow is water moving the wrong way, from a private irrigation system into the drinking supply. A backflow preventer on irrigation lines stops it. Many providers require annual testing by a certified tester, and failed devices have to be repaired or replaced.

If your lawn system ties into potable water at all, the backflow device is the safeguard. The page on backflow testing and prevention explains the device types and the yearly test.

Identify

How to tell which line is which

Look for labels. Non-potable lines and valve boxes are often marked, and the pipe or its tape is commonly purple. Outdoor spigots in some communities carry a warning tag. If nothing is marked, the HOA manager or the closing documents will say whether your community has a separate irrigation system.

When in doubt, treat an outdoor spigot as non-drinking water. The cost of the wrong guess is a stomach ache, and the cost of the right habit is nothing.

Mistakes

Common mistakes with irrigation water

  • ✓Connecting a non-potable hose or line to an indoor faucet or a drinking source.
  • ✓Skipping the yearly backflow test because the lawn looks fine.
  • ✓Calling the Town for a problem that belongs to the HOA.
  • ✓Leaving an irrigation line connected to a hose bib over winter.
  • ✓Assuming a soggy lawn is a potable leak when it may be a non-potable one.
Winterizing

Fall shutdown, in detail

Irrigation lines are drained or blown out with compressed air before the first hard freeze so water left in them cannot freeze and split a pipe. In a shared system, the HOA or metro district schedules that. In a private zone, a contractor or the homeowner does it.

Leave the controller off, drain the backflow device and disconnect any hoses. A line that was missed shows up in spring as a geyser. If you see one, close the zone valve if you can and call the HOA before assuming the potable system is at fault.

Clues

Reading your own irrigation clues

A zone that runs weak often has a broken head or a leak downstream. A soggy patch that never dries points to a buried leak. A pressure drop across the whole system suggests a main problem that belongs to the HOA. Walk the zones in daylight, with the system running, and photograph what you see.

Photos and notes make the HOA's repair faster, and they help a plumber if the leak turns out to be in the potable line after all.

Local note

Non-potable water in Windsor neighborhoods

Many Windsor neighborhoods irrigate with non-potable water, and the purple pipes and signs mark it. Whether your lot is on that system depends on the street and the HOA.

Ask your HOA or the Town before connecting anything to an irrigation line. If a pipe is unmarked and you are unsure, do not cut into it. Nearby pages: Severance and Timnath. Related service: backflow testing.

Local

Where this shows up in practice

The pattern is most common in the newer parts of town and in surrounding growth areas such as Severance. A summer bill spike or a soggy lawn is the common signal. The first question is whether the water is potable or non-potable, because that decides who owns the repair. If you are not sure, your closing documents or your HOA manager can tell you.
