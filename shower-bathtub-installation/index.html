Shower & Bathtub Installation in Windsor, CO

New tubs and showers, tub-to-shower conversions, valve replacements and drain upgrades. The plumbing is sized, permitted and tested, and the finish work is scoped separately.

  • Tub and shower installs
  • Valve replacement
  • Drain and vent work
  • Permit guidance
Symptoms

A worn tub, a shower that fluctuates in temperature, or a drain that backs up

A cracked or stained tub, a shower that scalds or runs cold when someone flushes a toilet, a handle that will not hold temperature, and a tub that drains slowly are the common reasons for this work. A single valve or drain problem is a repair, and an old tub or a layout change is an installation.

Shower and bathtub installation covers setting a new tub or shower pan, installing the valve and trim, connecting the drain and overflow, and testing everything before the walls are finished. Swapping a tub in an existing alcove is simpler than moving the drain or the valve. A shower that drips behind the wall is a leak detection question before it becomes a remodel question.

The International Plumbing Code (IPC) calls for pressure-balancing, thermostatic or combination valves in showers and tub-showers to limit scalding when other fixtures are used. The valve choice is made before the walls close, since changing it later means opening them again.

Process

What a tub or shower installation involves

  1. 1

    Plan and permit

    The Town of Windsor requires a building permit for new plumbing, and a fixture change or addition falls under that. Ask whether yours does.

  2. 2

    Rough-in

    Supply lines, valve body, drain and vent are set to the new layout.

  3. 3

    Set the tub or pan

    The tub or pan is leveled and supported, and the drain is connected and tested.

  4. 4

    Test before finishing

    Supply and drain are pressure- and leak-tested before walls and tile go on.

  5. 5

    Install trim

    The valve trim, showerhead and drain cover are installed once surfaces are finished.

Windsor

Bathroom details for Windsor homes

Federal rules cap showerheads at 2.5 gallons per minute. Windsor's mix of older homes and newer builds means older tubs may have galvanized or corroded drain pipe behind them, and newer showers often have plastic valves. A plumber should look inside the wall before choosing the scope.

Basement bathrooms may need an ejector pump instead of gravity drainage, and shower drains in basements deserve a trap that will not dry out. A shower or tub that drains slowly after cleaning is a sign of a line clog, covered under drain cleaning.

Before the work

What to do before the plumber arrives

  • ✓Clear the bathroom and cover nearby floors.
  • ✓Find the main shutoff and confirm it turns.
  • ✓Photograph the existing tub, valve and any wall stains.
  • ✓Decide on fixtures before the rough-in, since the valve depends on the trim.
  • ✓Plan where you will shower while the water is off.
