The utility

How Greeley's water and sewer system shapes plumbing calls

Greeley's drinking water comes from four river basins and is treated at the Boyd Lake and Bellevue plants. The same City supplies about 30 percent of the water the Town of Windsor buys. Greeley's Water and Sewer Department runs the mains, so a problem in the street goes to the City and a problem inside your property line is yours.

Greeley also runs an active cross-connection control program. A notice printed in the Town of Windsor's 2025 water report says Greeley's backflow testing ratio fell short in 2023 and returned to compliance by August 31, 2024. For a business, that is a reminder that backflow testing is enforced, covered under backflow testing and prevention.

History

A city that was built around water

The Union Colony, founded in 1869 and 1870 by Nathan Meeker, made water its first priority. Early residents drew from the Cache la Poudre River, Union Colony Ditch No. 3 and shallow wells, and in 1889 the city built a waterworks with a standpipe near Island Grove. The system has grown for over a century, which is also why the oldest homes in the area are here.

For a homeowner, the point is age. A house from the early 1900s can have original drain lines and a mix of repairs, and sewer line replacement is a real option when a camera shows failure along the run. From 80631 and 80634 to the campus ZIP, 80639, and nearby 80620 and 80550, repiping, sewer line replacement and commercial plumbing are the jobs to ask about first.

Floods

The river and the 2013 flood

The September 2013 flood put the Cache la Poudre and the South Platte into low-lying parts of Greeley, with hundreds of people under evacuation orders as water reached 11 feet. The lasting lesson for homeowners is to check sump pumps, backflow valves and flood coverage. Standard homeowner policies usually exclude flood, so a separate policy is the answer.

Before we arrive

Burst Pipe or Active Leak in Greeley: Do This Before We Arrive

Greeley calls range from century-old bungalows to restaurants after closing. The first four steps are the same for both.

1

Close the main valve

In an older Greeley home the shutoff can be in a basement, a crawl space or a utility closet. Turn it until the flow stops, and for a business, close the building's main as well.

2

Kill power to wet areas

Switch off breakers for wet rooms and any equipment sitting in water, such as a water heater or a dishwasher. If water is at the panel, stay clear.

3

Clear the area

Move stock, rugs and electronics off wet floors, and keep people out of any room with sewage. Take photos before cleanup for your insurer.

4

Call the 24/7 line
