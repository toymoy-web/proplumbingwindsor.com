Symptoms

Taste, smell, cloudiness, or a worry about what is in the water

People look into filtration after noticing a chlorine smell, a metallic or earthy taste, cloudiness, or after reading a water quality report. Tap water in the area is regulated, and filtration is about matching a treatment to a specific concern, not a rescue from unsafe water.

Water filtration installation means choosing and installing equipment for that concern: carbon for taste and chlorine, reverse osmosis for dissolved solids, a lead-certified filter for lead, and sediment filters for particles. The right match starts with a water test and a clear idea of what you want removed.

Types

Filter types and what each is for

Filters are only as good as their certification. NSF/ANSI 42 covers taste and chlorine, 53 covers health-related contaminants including lead, and 58 covers reverse osmosis. Match the standard to your concern, and replace cartridges on schedule.

Good for

  • Under-sink carbon: taste, chlorine and some contaminants at one tap
  • Reverse osmosis: dissolved solids, many kinds of contaminants, drinking water
  • Whole-home carbon: chlorine for showers, laundry and every tap
  • Sediment filter: particles and cloudiness

Limits

  • Carbon does not remove dissolved minerals or hardness
  • Reverse osmosis wastes water and needs drain space
  • Whole-home units need maintenance and flow sizing
  • No filter fixes a lead pipe, so replace the source

Why a local report matters more than an online claim

The Town of Windsor purchases water from other systems, about 30 percent from the City of Greeley and about 70 percent from Fort Collins-Loveland Water District and North Weld County Water District. Each supplier publishes its own water quality report, and the Town attaches theirs to its own. Read the report for your address, then pick a filter for what it actually lists.

Be wary of pages that quote alarming numbers for Windsor without citing a report. The Town's own report shows system-wide lead and byproduct results under their limits for 2024, so filters are an optional upgrade for taste, odor and personal preference. A hardness complaint is a different problem, covered under water softener installation and repair.

Installation

What a filtration installation involves

  1. 1

    Identify the concern

    Taste, odor, lead or sediment sets the filter type.

  2. 2

    Check space and water pressure

    Under-sink space, drain access and pressure decide what fits. Low pressure is worth a look at the supply piping first, covered under repiping.

  3. 3

    Install the unit

    Filter, shutoff and, for RO, a separate faucet and drain connection are installed.

  4. 4

    Flush and test

    New cartridges are flushed, leaks are checked, and flow is tested.

  5. 5

    Set a schedule

    Cartridge changes are scheduled to the manufacturer's interval.

Choosing safely

What to do before choosing a filter for lead

  • ✓Read the water quality report for your provider and address.
  • ✓Look for the NSF/ANSI 53 mark, and read what it certifies.
  • ✓Use cold water for drinking and cooking, and run the tap if it has sat.
  • ✓Replace cartridges on the maker's schedule.
  • ✓Ask your provider about a service line inventory, since a filter does not remove a lead pipe.
