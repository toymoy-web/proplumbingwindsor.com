Scope

Commercial plumbing calls in Windsor

Business plumbing differs from home plumbing in volume, hours and rules. A restroom that cannot be used closes a shop, a floor drain that backs up shuts a kitchen, and a leak in a shared wall involves a landlord and a neighboring tenant. Calls are scoped around keeping the business open.

Windsor's commercial base runs from older buildings on Main Street to the industrial development that has grown on the southeast side of town this century. Each end has its own plumbing problems.

Town rules

What the Town of Windsor requires for commercial work

The Town requires every business and industry on Town of Windsor water to have its backflow devices tested each year by a certified tester, with results sent to the Water Department. That covers devices on service mains, fire suppression lines, process lines and sprinkler supply lines. Cross-connection protection itself is a plumbing code subject, covered by the International Plumbing Code (IPC).

Changes to plumbing inside a commercial building also need plans. The Town's commercial submittal checklist calls for mechanical and plumbing plans stamped by a Colorado licensed engineer, and plumbing plans are required for any change to sewers, drains, vents, water piping, roof drains or gas piping inside a building.

That is why small repairs and larger alterations are scoped differently. A like-for-like fixture swap is service work. Moving drains or adding fixtures starts with plans and a permit, so raise it early.

After hours

Commercial plumbing emergencies

Burst lines, sewer backups and water heater failures do not wait for business hours. The line is answered 24/7, and an emergency call starts with stopping the water and keeping people out of affected areas.

If a leak has soaked flooring or ceiling tiles, leak detection finds the source and water damage restoration handles the drying. Photograph everything for the insurer before cleanup starts.

Emergency

Emergency commercial leaks, floor drains and lift pumps

Businesses call at all hours for overflowing floor drains, failed lift pumps and leaks in shopping plazas or parking areas. The 24/7 line is answered for commercial sites, and the first step is shutting off the affected branch or the building main.

Multi-tenant buildings add a question of whose line it is, so note the unit and the shutoff location when you call. Grease trap pump-outs are normally done by a hauler, and we can tell you quickly whether the problem is the trap or the line.

Before you call

What to do first in a commercial plumbing emergency

  • ✓Close the water shutoff for the affected area, or the building main if you cannot isolate it.
  • ✓Keep staff and customers out of wet areas, and put out signs where floors are slick.
  • ✓Stay away from outlets, panels and equipment that water has reached.
  • ✓Photograph the damage and the source before cleanup, for the owner and the insurer.
  • ✓Note the suite, the fixture or line involved, and who approves repair cost.
  • ✓If sewage is involved, close the area and do not use fixtures on that line.
