Safety first

If you smell gas, do this before anything else

Natural gas has an added odor that smells like rotten eggs. A gas smell is never a wait-for-the-weekend problem, and it is the one case where the plumber is the second call and not the first.

  • ✓Leave the house immediately with everyone, including pets.
  • ✓Do not flip light switches, use a lighter, or start a car in the garage.
  • ✓Call 911 and your gas utility from outside or a neighbor's house.
  • ✓Do not re-enter until the utility says it is safe.
  • ✓Call a plumber for repairs only after the utility clears the leak.
What it is

What gas line work covers

Gas line installation runs new piping from the meter or an existing branch to an appliance, sized for the appliance's load and the length of the run. Repair fixes leaks, damaged sections and old connections. Either way, the work ends with a pressure test and a leak check before gas is turned on.

Common projects are kitchen ranges, clothes dryers, outdoor grills, fire pits and patio heaters, and water heaters, including the conversion from a tank to a tankless unit that needs a larger line, covered under tankless water heater service. Each of these needs its own pipe size, which is worked out from the appliance load and the length of the run.

Materials

Gas piping materials and where each is used

Gas piping materials, where each is used and key rules
MaterialTypical useKey rule
Black iron pipeIndoor main runs and appliance branchesThreaded joints, sealed with gas-rated compound, tested before use
CSST (flexible stainless tubing)Retrofit runs through framingMust be bonded to the electrical system, per the manufacturer and code
Coated copper or approved plasticUnderground outdoor runs where code allowsDepth, tracer wire and material approvals matter
Flexible appliance connectorsThe last few feet to a range or dryerApproved for the appliance, never run through walls

Installers follow the International Fuel Gas Code (IFGC) and the Town's adopted codes, and CSST bonding follows the National Electrical Code (NEC) and the manufacturer's instructions. A job that does not pass inspection is not finished.

Process

What a gas line installation involves

  1. 1

    Size the line

    Appliance load in BTUs, the length of the run and the existing supply determine pipe size.

  2. 2

    Permit

    The Town of Windsor requires a building permit to install or move plumbing, and gas piping is part of that review. Ask whether a plan is needed.

  3. 3

    Locate underground utilities

    Colorado requires a call to 811 at least two business days before digging for an outdoor run.

  4. 4

    Install and support

    Piping is run, supported and, for CSST, bonded. A shutoff valve and a sediment trap are added at the appliance.

  5. 5

    Test and inspect

    The line is pressure-tested and checked for leaks, then inspected before gas is turned on.

Windsor

Gas line details that matter in Windsor

Windsor sits at about 4,800 feet. Gas appliances need to be set up for altitude per the manufacturer, and orifice or settings changes are the installer's job, not an afterthought. A new range or tankless heater installed with sea-level settings can burn poorly.

Many Windsor homes have an outdoor kitchen or fire pit in the backyard. A buried line there needs the right material, depth and locates, and it is a separate scope from a kitchen range. Hookups near a water heater often tie into water heater installation and replacement planning.

Pricing

What drives the price of gas line work

A short extension to a range costs less than a long buried run to a fire pit. The price is explained before work starts.
