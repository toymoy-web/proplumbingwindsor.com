Emergency first steps when a pipe bursts

Water from a burst line keeps coming until the supply is closed, so the first minutes matter more than the phone call. Work through the steps below in order.

  • ✓Close the main shutoff valve, usually where the water line enters the house.
  • ✓Open a faucet on the lowest floor to drain the remaining water from the line.
  • ✓Turn off the water heater power or gas if the burst is on the hot side.
  • ✓Stay away from outlets, cords and the panel if water has reached them.
  • ✓Move belongings off the floor, and photograph the damage before cleanup.
What it is

What a burst pipe repair involves

Burst pipe repair isolates the failed section, cuts out the damaged pipe, installs a new section with proper fittings, and pressure-tests the line before the water is turned back on. Most bursts are a split along the length of the pipe or a failed joint, and the repair is a section replacement and not a patch.

If the cause is a freeze, the neighboring pipe is often weakened too, and a thaw can reveal more than one split. That case is covered under frozen pipe repair.

Causes

Why pipes burst, and what each cause means for the repair

CauseHow it shows upRepair note
FreezingA split along the pipe after a cold snap, often in a garage, exterior wall or crawl spaceReplace the split section and check nearby runs
High water pressureRepeated leaks and joint failures, with a hammering soundReplace the failed part and test or replace the pressure reducing valve

Water hammerBanging when a valve closes quickly, then a failed jointRepair the joint and add hammer arrestors
Shifting groundA break near a foundation corner or slab edgeReplace the section and reduce stress on the line

The International Plumbing Code (IPC) limits static water pressure in a building to 80 psi, which is why a home with a missing or failed pressure reducing valve can burst joints without any freeze. A pressure reducing valve with a gauge costs little next to the repair bill from a burst joint.

Windsor

Burst pipes in Windsor homes

Windsor's building code puts the frost line at 30 inches, so buried mains are normally below freezing depth. The pipes that burst are above it: hose bibs, garage and exterior wall runs, and crawl space lines. Windsor's weather swings hard, with Chinook winds that can push temperatures near 70 degrees in January and then drop them fast, and pipes that thawed overnight can freeze by the next morning.

Basements raise the stakes. A burst above a finished basement soaks ceilings, insulation and carpet below, which is where water damage restoration comes in for drying. Knowing where the main shutoff is before an emergency is the single most useful preparation.

Homeowner policies commonly cover the water damage from a sudden burst, though the repair of the pipe itself is often the owner's cost. Call your insurer early and keep the failed section if it is easy to do so.
