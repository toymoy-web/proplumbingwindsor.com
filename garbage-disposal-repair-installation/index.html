Symptoms

Humming, silence, leaks or a sink that will not drain

A hum with no grinding means the blades are jammed. A silent unit with no hum is often a tripped reset or a power issue. Water at the bottom points to a leak at a seal or flange, and a sink that drains slowly after the disposal runs usually means a clog past it.

Garbage disposal repair covers clearing a jam, resetting overloads, replacing seals and flanges, and replacing a worn-out unit. Installation covers a new disposal with the dishwasher hookup, drain connection and power. A disposal that hums but will not drain can also point to a clog further down the line, which is a drain cleaning question and not a disposal one.

Faults

Disposal faults and fixes

A disposal is a small motor spinning a flywheel with impellers, not blades. Hard objects jam the flywheel, and water seeps past seals as they age. Telling a jam from a dead motor is the first step.

Garbage disposal faults, their usual causes and the usual fix
SymptomUsual causeFix
Hums, does not spinJammed flywheelUnplug, then turn the flywheel with an Allen wrench from underneath
SilentTripped overload or no powerPress the red reset, check the outlet and switch
Leaks from the bottomCracked housing or failed sealReplace the unit
Leaks at the sink flangeLoose mounting or old puttyReset the flange with fresh seal
Sink drains slowlyClog in the trap or line past the disposalClear the line, see drain cleaning

Most disposals last 8 to 15 years. A leaking housing means replacement, because the seal cannot be repaired from outside.

Installation

What a disposal installation involves

  1. 1

    Choose horsepower

    One-third to three-quarter horsepower is common for homes, and higher for heavy use.

  2. 2

    Remove the old unit

    Power is off, and the drain, dishwasher hose and mounting ring are released.

  3. 3

    Mount the new unit

    The mounting assembly and flange are sealed and the disposal is hung.

  4. 4

    Connect drain and dishwasher

    The drain tailpiece and, if present, the dishwasher hose are connected.

  5. 5

    Wire and test

    Power is connected to a switched outlet or cord, and the unit is tested for leaks and noise.

Windsor

Disposal details for Windsor kitchens

The International Plumbing Code (IPC) treats a disposal as a fixture that needs its own trap and a sound connection to the drain. Grease is the main enemy of a kitchen drain. Windsor's older neighborhoods with mature lines are especially sensitive, and a disposal does not prevent grease from hardening in the line downstream. Pour grease into a can, and run cold water while grinding.

If the kitchen drain keeps clogging, the line past the disposal may be the real issue, and recurring kitchen clogs often point toward hydro jetting. Replacing a disposal is routine work, and the Town's permit technician at 970-674-2436 can say whether a permit applies to a new installation.

Safety

What to do before working near a disposal

  • ✓Turn off the wall switch and unplug the unit or switch off its breaker.
  • ✓Never put a hand or tool into the grinding chamber.
  • ✓Use tongs or pliers to remove a stuck object.
  • ✓Use the Allen wrench socket at the bottom to free a jam.
  • ✓Keep the area dry, and do not use drain cleaners in a disposal.
