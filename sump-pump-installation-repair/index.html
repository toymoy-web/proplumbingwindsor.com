Urgent

The pit is full, the pump runs nonstop, or it will not turn on

A sump pump failure is an emergency when it happens during a storm or snowmelt, because water rises in the pit and then in the basement. The pump may be dead, jammed with debris, stuck on its float, or simply without power. Each has a different fix.

Sump pump installation and repair covers the pump, the float switch, the check valve and the discharge line that carries water outside. A sump pump sits in a pit below the basement floor and moves groundwater that collects there away from the foundation.

Troubleshooting

What common sump pump problems mean

Sump pump symptoms, their usual causes and typical fixes
SymptomUsual causeTypical fix
Pit full, pump silentPower outage, tripped breaker, dead motor or stuck floatCheck power, then the float, then replace the pump if the motor is dead
Pump runs constantlyStuck float, failed check valve or a very high water tableFree the float and test the check valve, then look at capacity
Pump cycles on and off rapidlyCheck valve not holding, so water drains back into the pitReplace the check valve
Pump runs but moves little waterClogged intake or impeller, or a blocked or frozen dischargeClear the intake and the discharge line
Loud grinding or rattlingWorn bearings or debris in the impellerReplace the pump
Pump runs but the pit keeps fillingPump undersized for the inflowCompare capacity and consider a second pump

Backup power is the gap that fails most households. Storms that raise groundwater are often the storms that knock out power, so a pump with no backup may stop when it is needed most. Basement moisture beyond the pit is a basement plumbing question.

Safety

Emergency steps while water is rising in the basement

  • ✓Do not stand in water near the pump, its cord, outlets or the electrical panel.
  • ✓If water has reached outlets or the panel, stay out and call your electric utility or an electrician.
  • ✓Move belongings and stored items up off the floor.
  • ✓Do not use a household vacuum on standing water.
  • ✓Clear leaves and debris from the discharge outlet outside, if you can do it safely.
  • ✓Photograph the water level and damage for your insurer.

Windsor rules

Where sump pump water is allowed to go in Windsor

Windsor Municipal Code section 7-2-50 says no water may be discharged from a sump pump so that it drains onto or across any driveway, sidewalk, curb, gutter or street. That rules out the common shortcut of pointing the discharge at the street.

Section 13-1-50 separately bars groundwater, roof runoff and subsurface drainage from any sanitary sewer, and the code forbids connecting exterior foundation drains or areaway drains to a building sewer or drain that connects to the sanitary sewer. The discharge needs a lawful outlet, such as the yard away from the foundation, and the Town's engineering office at 970-674-2400 can say what is accepted at your address.

Groundwater is the other local factor. The Town's flood insurance study ties major flooding in the Poudre valley to intense local rain combined with snowmelt, and spring snowmelt and rain are when sump pumps work hardest.

Basement bathrooms add a different pump. The Town's sewer rules say that where a building drain is too low for gravity flow to the public sewer, the sewage must be lifted by an approved means. That is a sewage ejector pump, which is not a sump pump and must never be piped like one.

Windsor's sump calls come from places like Water Valley, the homes near Windsor Lake and Boardwalk Park, and the older blocks close to the Cache la Poudre River at Eastman Park. Ground sitting nearer to a lake or river can hold more water after a storm, so those basements are worth checking before the wet season.

Installation

What a new sump pump installation involves

  1. 1

    Check the pit and the inflow

    The pit size, its depth and how fast water arrives set the pump size.

  2. 2

    Choose the pump

    Submersible pumps sit in the pit and run quietly. Pedestal pumps sit above it and are easier to service. Common residential pumps run at about one-third to one-half horsepower.

  3. 3

    Set the float and check valve

    The float switch is placed so the pump starts before water reaches the slab, and a check valve stops water from draining back.

  4. 4

    Route the discharge

    The discharge line runs to a lawful outlet away from the foundation, and not across a sidewalk or onto the street.

  5. 5

    Add backup power and test

    A battery or water-powered backup is connected, and the system is tested by filling the pit.

Pricing

What changes the price of sump pump work

A pump swap in an existing pit is the smallest job. A new pit, a longer discharge run or a backup system adds scope.

Pump typeSubmersible, pedestal and horsepower change the price.
PitA new pit means cutting the slab, and an existing one does not.
Discharge routeA longer or buried run adds labor.
Backup systemBattery and water-powered units price differently.
Time of callStorm nights and weekends can price differently.

Standard homeowner policies commonly exclude water that backs up through sump pumps or drains unless an endorsement adds it. Check your policy before the wet season, and photograph the pit and pump now.
