Seasons

The Johnstown plumbing calendar

Johnstown sits between the Big Thompson and Little Thompson rivers, and its plumbing year follows the water. Spring runoff and May-to-August cloudbursts stress sump pumps and basements. Summer irrigation season brings leaks and backflow tests. Winter cold brings frozen hose bibs and garage lines.

The river setting is not abstract. During the September 2013 flood, a Colorado Division of Dams study put the flow down the Little Thompson at about 52,000 acre-feet between September 10 and 16. That is why a failed sump pump is a first-order emergency here.

History

Old Main Street and the newer subdivisions

Johnstown grew along the Great Western Railway. A 1911 fire destroyed the wooden buildings on west Main Street, and they were rebuilt in brick. Those older buildings have older drains, and commercial calls there lean toward commercial plumbing. The town now has about 17,300 residents, with most of its homes in newer subdivisions such as Pioneer Ridge, Thompson Ranch Junction and Rolling Hills Ranch.

Newer homes often have basements, sump systems and a water heater in the utility room. The practical check is whether the sump pump has a backup and a lawful discharge, and whether the water heater has a drain pan.

Utilities

Johnstown's utility setup

The Town of Johnstown has long run its own water and wastewater systems, according to state water board filings, with raw water brought through the Home Supply Ditch into Johnstown Reservoir. Little Thompson Water District serves areas nearby. Johnstown lies in both Larimer and Weld counties, so confirm your address and provider before assuming whose main is involved. Rules and prep

Preparing a Johnstown basement before the wet season

A short check done in April beats a storm night. Pour a bucket of water into the sump pit and confirm the pump starts and the float rises and falls freely. Check that the discharge line runs well away from the foundation and is not frozen or blocked, and that the check valve clicks shut when the pump stops.

Rules about where a sump can discharge are set by each town. Windsor's code, for example, bars discharge across sidewalks, curbs and streets, and other towns have their own rules. Ask Johnstown before routing a line toward the street, and keep in mind that Greeley, the Weld County seat, has different rules again. Calls from 80534, 80538, 80537, 80513 and nearby 80543 reach one dispatcher, with sump pump repair and drain cleaning as the first services to ask about.

Storm night

If the Pit Overflows or a Pipe Bursts in Johnstown, Before We Arrive

Johnstown's wet nights come fast. These four moves protect the house until a crew reaches the door.

1

Close the main valve

Find the shutoff where the water line enters the house, often on a basement wall. Turn it until the flow stops, then open a low faucet to drain the line.

2

Cut power if the pit is rising

If water is climbing toward outlets, the furnace or the panel, stay out of the basement and call. Switch off breakers only from a dry spot.

3

Clear the floor

Lift boxes, rugs and electronics above the waterline and note how high the water reached. Photos of the level help with an insurance claim.

4

Call the 24/7 line
