Housing eras

Milliken's housing eras and what each one needs

Milliken was incorporated on October 1, 1910, when it annexed the neighboring community of Hillsboro. A building boom in the late 1960s and early 1970s then added a large number of affordable homes, and newer subdivisions followed as the population reached 8,386 in 2020. Each era has its own plumbing profile.

Homes from the boom decades are now past 50 years old. Their original water heaters are long gone, but their sewer lines, supply piping and shutoffs are often original. That is where sewer line replacement and repiping questions come from.

Weather

The Little Thompson and Milliken's cloudbursts

The Little Thompson River flows along the north side of town. A flood study for Milliken found that the main cause of flooding there is cloudbursts, which usually occur from May through August, in a place that averages 12.2 inches of precipitation a year. A dry year does not remove the risk, because heavy rain can arrive all at once.

For homes, the practical issue is groundwater and drainage. A sump pump with backup power is the first defense for a basement. Sewer backups during storms point to the same rainfall problem finding its way into drains.

Utilities

Water and sewer in Milliken

By 2004 Milliken had added to its potable water sources and more than doubled the capacity of its sewer system, and those upgrades supported a wave of new housing. The Town now serves about 8,400 people. Confirm with the Town which main serves your street before assuming a blockage is the Town's.

While you wait

Active Leak in a Milliken Home? Before We Arrive, Do This

Older Milliken houses reward a calm first five minutes. Work through these in order.

1

Shut off the main

In a 1970s ranch the shutoff is often in the basement or crawl space near the front wall. Turn it until the water stops, and close the water heater's cold inlet valve too if the tank is leaking.

2

Kill power to wet areas

Turn off breakers for wet rooms, and for the water heater if it is the source. If a sewer backup is touching outlets or the panel, stay out and call.

3

Clear the area

Stop using sinks, toilets and the washing machine if a drain is backing up. Keep children and pets out of sewage, and photograph the damage first.

4

Upgrades

Upgrades that pay off in a 1970s Milliken home

Three upgrades come up again and again in houses from the boom decades. The first is the sewer line, where a camera look decides between waiting and replacing. The second is the water heater drain pan and relief valve discharge, which many original installs lack. The third is the shutoff valves under sinks and toilets, which stick or crumble when turned after decades, and the International Plumbing Code (IPC) now expects one on every fixture supply.

Milliken shares the Big Thompson valley with Johnstown, and the two towns see similar basement and drainage calls. The difference is mostly housing age, which the camera and the shutoff valves reveal quickly. Calls from 80543, 80634 and 80651, plus nearby 80534 and 80620, go to the same dispatcher, with sewer line repair, water heater repair and supply line work as the services to ask about first.
