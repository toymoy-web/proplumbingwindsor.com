Dripping handles, weak flow, leaks under the cabinet and slow sink drains. The failing part is identified first, so you only replace what has worn out.

Symptoms

A drip, a leak under the cabinet, or flow that has faded

A dripping spout, a handle that leaks at its base, weak flow, a loose faucet, a cabinet floor that stays damp, and a sink that drains slowly are the common faults. Most come from a worn internal part or a loose connection, not a failed faucet.

Faucet and sink repair means finding which part is worn: the cartridge, valve seat, O-rings, supply line or drain trap. Replacing the part is often cheaper than replacing the faucet.

Faucet types

Faucet types and why each one drips

Knowing the type matters because the repair kit differs. A single-handle faucet is usually a cartridge, and a two-handle faucet is usually a washer and seat. Matching the part to the type is what keeps the repair from being repeated in a month.

Faucet types, the usual cause of dripping and the usual fix
TypeUsual cause of a dripUsual fix
Compression (two handles)Worn rubber washer or seatReplace the washer and seat
CartridgeWorn cartridge or O-ringsReplace the cartridge
BallWorn seats, springs or O-ringsReplace the seals kit
Ceramic discWorn seals or mineral buildupClean or replace the cylinder

The International Plumbing Code (IPC) requires an accessible shutoff on each fixture supply, so a failed shutoff is a real repair. A faucet more than 15 or 20 years old with a corroded body is usually better replaced than repaired. A leak that returns under the cabinet after a new supply line points to the shutoff valve or the trap, and a stubborn drip behind a wall is a job for leak detection.

Emergency

Emergency repair for burst supply lines and failed valves

A burst supply line or a failed shutoff valve under a sink can flood a kitchen in minutes. Close the angle stop under the fixture, or the main valve if the stop will not turn, and call the 24/7 line.

Fixture work is quick once the water is off, because a supply line or cartridge is a standard part. Dripping faucets and slow flow are scheduled repairs, and the price is explained before we start.

Quick checks

What to check before you call

  • ✓Close the small shutoff valves under the sink and see whether the leak stops.
  • ✓Check the supply line connections for drips with a dry paper towel.
  • ✓Look at the drain trap and slip nuts for moisture.
  • ✓Unscrew the aerator and rinse out grit if flow is weak.
  • ✓Note whether the leak is hot or cold side.
Windsor

What Windsor homes see

Windsor homes include newer builds with builder-grade faucets and older homes with decades-old fixtures. Hard-water scale can clog aerators, and the right answer depends on your supplier's water. Flow rules matter too: federal rules cap bathroom faucets at 2.2 gallons per minute, and WaterSense models use 1.5, which affects replacement choices.

A slow sink drain can be a trap clog or something deeper. If several fixtures are slow, the problem is further down the line and belongs to drain cleaning. Ask the Town's permit technician at 970-674-2436 whether a fixture swap needs a permit, since adding a new fixture generally does.
